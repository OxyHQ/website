## Key takeaways

<Takeaways>

- Oxy exists to build practical alternatives to systems that exploit people. Technology is its first instrument, not its final boundary.
- Oxy can be a business and can create financial value. What it cannot do is turn people into the product or let capital become the final authority over its purpose.
- Some commitments are not for sale: human dignity, privacy, no advertising based extraction, meaningful access, honest design, open source, the freedom to leave, and power with limits.
- Ethical leadership cannot depend on one good person staying good forever. A trustworthy institution limits power through its design.
- Identity should give people control rather than become a tool for surveillance, and no universal score should decide whether a person deserves housing, food, education or respect.
- This is a founding draft, not a finished institution. Its purpose is to make the direction clear enough that people can decide whether to trust it, join it and hold it accountable.

</Takeaways>

## Preamble

Oxy began with a simple refusal. At twelve years old, Nate Isern Álvarez looked at the advertising, data collection and artificial restrictions that had become normal online and felt that digital life should not work that way. He did not begin with a business plan or a theory of institutions. He began by trying to build a better social network.

The project grew because the original problem was not isolated. The same pattern appeared again and again. Technology treated people as inventory. Markets rewarded extraction. Platforms concentrated power in the hands of a few owners. Housing, education, culture, economic opportunity and public life often became harder to access for the people who needed them most. Oxy gradually became a response to that wider reality.

Oxy has a legal corporate form because servers, contracts, employment, infrastructure and responsibility require one. Its purpose, however, is larger than the form used to operate it. Oxy is intended to become an open institution and civic ecosystem that creates, runs and supports ethical alternatives.

Oxy accepts that a durable mission needs income. People deserve fair pay. Infrastructure has real costs. Artists, developers, researchers, support teams and communities cannot build lasting work through exhaustion alone. Oxy can be a business and can create financial value. What it cannot do is turn people into the product, sell the mission to the highest bidder or allow capital to become the final authority over its purpose.

Oxy also rejects the idea that ethical leadership can depend on one good person remaining good forever. Founders change. Leaders change. Wealth and power change the conditions around them. A trustworthy institution must therefore limit power through its design. Decisions should be visible, contestable and distributed. No founder, executive, investor or administrator should be able to change the rights of others on personal impulse.

This charter is not a complete technical specification, a set of corporate bylaws or a claim that every aspiration has already been achieved. It is a moral constitution for the work ahead.<ArticleCitation id="document-status">1</ArticleCitation>

## 1. Purpose before scale

The primary purpose of Oxy is to improve life in the real world. Technology, revenue, reach, reputation and institutional size matter only when they help people live with greater dignity, safety, freedom and opportunity.

Oxy exists in response to conditions that should not be accepted as inevitable. People go hungry while resources are wasted. People remain without secure housing while wealth accumulates without purpose. Digital services ask for personal data, attention and dependence in exchange for basic participation. Communities are governed by systems they cannot inspect and decisions they cannot appeal. Oxy will not solve every injustice at once, but the scale of a problem must never become an excuse for doing nothing.

User numbers, revenue and valuation can help measure capacity. They are not the final measure of success. Oxy succeeds when a person is safer because a system respected them, when a creator can participate without surrendering control, when a family gains access to something previously out of reach, when a community can make a decision fairly and when a useful service remains accountable to the people who depend on it.

Growth is welcome when it increases that impact. Growth that weakens dignity, privacy, fairness or public accountability is not success.

## 2. What Oxy is

Oxy is an institution in formation. It is also a community, an open source ecosystem, a business, a shared identity layer and a growing family of practical initiatives. None of these descriptions is sufficient alone.

Oxy is not simply a collection of applications. The applications are current expressions of a common purpose. They address different parts of life, but they are connected by identity, infrastructure, values and a belief that people deserve alternatives to systems built around extraction.

Oxy is not only a charity. It may provide direct help, enable mutual aid, operate commercial services, build infrastructure and create sustainable businesses. Ethical work is not made less ethical because it earns revenue. The question is how that revenue is created, who bears the cost and who has power over the result.

Oxy is not a conventional startup whose mission can be rewritten after a financing round or acquisition. It is not a personality cult around its founder. It is not a state seeking coercive authority over people. Its long term ambition is to become a voluntary and trusted institution capable of carrying responsibilities that may resemble those of cooperatives, civic networks and public services, while remaining accountable to the people it serves.

Every legal entity, product, protocol and commercial activity operating under the Oxy name should be understood as a steward of this purpose rather than its owner.

## 3. Commitments that are not for sale

Some choices can change as technology, culture and law evolve. The following commitments define the identity of Oxy and must not be traded away for convenience or capital.

### 3.1 Human dignity

Every person has value beyond their account, income, productivity, popularity, reputation or purchasing power. Oxy must never design systems that confuse commercial value with human worth.

Safety systems, trust signals and moderation decisions may affect access to particular features when necessary, but they must remain proportionate, explainable and open to review. Basic dignity is not a reward that Oxy grants and cannot be removed by a score.

### 3.2 Privacy and personal data

People should know what information is collected, why it is needed, where it is stored and how it can be removed or moved. Oxy will not sell personal data, trade it for commercial advantage or repurpose it for behavioural advertising.

Data may be processed when required to deliver a service, protect users, prevent abuse, maintain security or comply with law. That necessity must not become a pretext for unlimited collection. The preferred design is to collect less, retain less and place meaningful control in the hands of the person concerned.

### 3.3 No advertising based extraction

Oxy will not finance its core services by selling access to human attention. It will not place commercial advertising inside its products as a condition of ordinary participation, and it will not build engagement systems whose purpose is to keep people present for the benefit of advertisers.

Discovery, recommendations and visibility must be designed around relevance, quality, consent and community value. Payment must not quietly become the ability to manipulate what other people see.

### 3.4 Meaningful access

A free service should be genuinely useful, not a deceptive preview designed to create dependency before essential functions are removed. Paid services may add capacity, convenience, professional tools, hosting, support or features with real operating costs. Payment must not be required for basic dignity, fair treatment, ownership of identity or the ability to leave with personal data.

Where possible, pricing should consider local conditions, accessibility and the needs of people with limited resources. Oxy should seek ways for communities, institutions and those with greater means to help sustain access for others.

### 3.5 Honest design

Oxy must not use dark patterns, hidden fees, confusing cancellation, false scarcity, artificial urgency or deliberately addictive loops. Interfaces should help people understand the consequences of a choice before they make it.

Clarity is a product feature. Consent is meaningful only when refusal is possible and understandable.

### 3.6 Open source and transparency

Core code that affects the public interest should be open by default when privacy, security and law allow. Open source is both an invitation to participate and a form of accountability. It allows people to inspect what a system claims to do, build upon it and challenge weaknesses.

Transparency does not mean exposing personal information, security secrets or details that would make abuse easier. It means publishing the rules, architecture, incentives and material limitations needed for informed trust.

### 3.7 Freedom to connect and leave

Oxy should favour open protocols, interoperability, data portability and compatible services. A person should not be trapped because their identity, relationships, work or history has been made impossible to move.

The right to leave is part of the right to stay freely. Loyalty that depends on lock in is not trust.

### 3.8 Power with limits

No person or group should hold unreviewable authority over the rights, speech, identity or livelihood of others inside Oxy. Emergency powers may sometimes be necessary, but they must be narrow, recorded, time limited and subject to later review.

The founder is not exempt. Investors are not exempt. Employees, community representatives, automated systems and future governing bodies are not exempt.

## 4. An ethical business that can endure

Oxy must be financially sustainable. A mission that cannot pay for infrastructure, security, maintenance and human work will eventually become dependent on someone whose interests may not be aligned with the people it serves.

Acceptable revenue can include optional premium services, managed hosting, technical support, professional tools, institutional plans, clearly disclosed transaction fees, ethical commerce, licensing that remains consistent with open source commitments, grants, donations, research partnerships and capital provided under mission protecting terms. The exact mix may change across initiatives.

The test is not whether money changes hands. The test is whether the exchange is clear, voluntary, proportionate and connected to real value or cost. Oxy should not create artificial obstacles and then charge people to remove them. It should not make safety, privacy or ordinary respect into luxury features.

Surplus should strengthen the institution. It may fund reserves, fair compensation, better infrastructure, access programmes, research, new initiatives and direct work in communities. Ethical work does not require poverty from the people doing it. Fair pay, rest and long term stability are part of the mission.

Oxy should publish enough information for people to understand how its main activities are funded and how financial incentives affect product decisions. It should be willing to explain not only what it earns, but why a particular model was chosen.

## 5. Capital may support the mission, never own it

Oxy may accept investment and may create a financial return for people and institutions that help it grow. It must not accept terms that give capital the right to abandon the commitments in this charter.

An investor must not be able to require advertising, the sale of personal data, manipulative growth, unfair pricing, the removal of open source commitments or unilateral control over moderation and governance. Money may purchase an agreed economic interest. It may not purchase moral authority over the people Oxy serves.

Oxy should favour structures that align patient capital with long term impact. Depending on the initiative and the law, these may include non voting investment, capped returns, revenue participation, mission locked shares, public benefit structures, grants, community ownership and independent stewardship. This charter defines the intended result rather than prescribing a single legal mechanism.

Material financing conditions that could affect the mission should be disclosed as far as law and legitimate confidentiality allow. A community cannot defend a promise it is not permitted to know exists.

The sentence that should guide every financing decision is simple: money may sustain the mission, but it must never be allowed to own it.

## 6. Governance designed for fallible people

Oxy must be designed on the assumption that every leader is fallible. Good intentions are valuable, but they are not a governance system. A promise that a founder will always behave well is not an adequate safeguard for millions of people.

Rules that affect users should be public and applied consistently. Important decisions should include reasons. People affected by a decision should have access to a meaningful appeal. Original decisions should not be silently rewritten in order to hide mistakes.

As Oxy grows, governance should include the people who use its services, the people who build and operate them, contributors, affected communities and independent stewards of the mission. Not every decision requires a public vote. Technical teams need room to work, and some security decisions require discretion. The distribution of power should match the consequences of the decision.

Constitutional matters require a higher standard than ordinary product choices. Changes to privacy, identity, funding, community rights, content governance or mission protection should not be made by a single executive or ordinary shareholder majority.

CrowdSource illustrates one direction for participatory decisions. Reports can become cases whose evidence is preserved, reviewed by temporary independent juries and resolved through versioned decisions. Appeals can create a new jury rather than erase the first record. Automation may organise evidence, translate and detect duplication, but it should not become the final judge.

Any governance model can be captured or manipulated. Oxy must test its systems, publish limitations, monitor unequal outcomes and remain willing to change mechanisms that fail while preserving the underlying rights they were meant to protect.

## 7. Identity, trust and reputation

Identity is the foundation of participation across Oxy, but identity must not become a tool for unnecessary surveillance. Commons and the shared Oxy identity layer are intended to give people control over credentials, signed records, sessions and the ways they prove claims about themselves.

Oxy may support verified identity, pseudonymous participation, proof of personhood and role based credentials as different concepts. They should not be collapsed into a single demand for a public legal name. A service should ask only for the level of assurance needed for the situation.

Trust and reputation can help communities make decisions, reduce abuse and recognise constructive participation. They can also become dangerous when they are opaque, permanent or treated as a measure of human value. Oxy must therefore keep reputation contextual, explainable, proportionate and open to appeal.

A reputation effect should follow evidence and a completed process, not accusation alone. Consequences should be bounded and should be capable of expiring, changing with new evidence or being repaired through later conduct. Delegated trust may create limited responsibility when someone repeatedly endorses harmful behaviour, but those effects must be visible and reversible rather than mysterious punishment.

No universal score should determine whether a person deserves housing, food, education, medical care, basic communication or respect. Trust tools exist to support specific decisions, not to rank the worth of a life.

## 8. Technology is an instrument, not the mission

Oxy should use technology when it creates genuine agency, access, safety or efficiency. It should not digitise a problem merely to appear innovative, and it should not treat an application as a substitute for material help.

The current architecture already points toward shared identity, signed records, user controlled data, federation and reusable public infrastructure. Mention can connect with the wider social web through ActivityPub. The Oxy platform includes a protocol for signed records and a personal data node that people can host for themselves. Commons owns the creation of self sovereign identity and cross device sign in. These are practical steps toward reducing dependence on a single application or server.

Decentralisation is a direction, not a slogan. Some components may remain centralised while safer and simpler alternatives are developed. Whenever centralisation is necessary, the dependency should be documented, the data should remain portable and the component should be designed so that it can be replaced without destroying the community around it.

Artificial intelligence should help people rather than harvest them. It can assist with accessibility, translation, discovery, support, spam reduction, safety analysis and creative work. It should not secretly manipulate behaviour or replace accountable human judgement in decisions that can seriously harm a person. Oxy may develop its own models over time to gain independence, but independence is valuable only when accompanied by safety, transparency and meaningful usefulness.

Technical sophistication is not a moral achievement by itself. The question is always what the system allows a person or community to do, and what power it removes from them in return.

## 9. One mission, many doors

Oxy initiatives meet people in different parts of life. Commons and Accounts provide identity and account control. Mention creates an open social space connected to the Fediverse. Allo develops communication. CrowdSource explores participatory moderation. Syra creates a home for music, artists, listeners and live experiences. Mercaria brings together shops, individuals, new goods and second hand exchange. Homiio addresses housing, profiles and trust. Alia, Codea, Astro and OxyOS pursue greater independence in artificial intelligence, software creation, browsing and personal computing. Economic initiatives can support fair exchange and local resilience.

These projects should not become isolated brands competing for the same limited attention. They are different doors into one institution. A person may first arrive as a listener, developer, tenant, seller, contributor, organiser or social user. The deeper invitation is the same: help build systems that respect people.

Not every initiative will mature at the same speed. Some will change names, merge, pause or be replaced. Oxy should be attached to the need and the principle, not to preserving every product forever.

Shared infrastructure should reduce duplication while allowing each initiative to develop the policies and culture appropriate to its domain. A housing service cannot be governed exactly like a music service, but both should honour privacy, fairness, accountability and user freedom.

## 10. A community that can build and govern

Oxy needs more than customers. It needs developers, maintainers, artists, writers, translators, researchers, sellers, residents, organisers, reviewers, educators, funders and people willing to challenge weak ideas before they become harmful systems.

Participation should be possible at many levels. A first contribution may be a translation, a bug report, a song, a local introduction, a design correction or one small piece of code. Tasks should be visible, documented and limited enough that a new person can complete something meaningful without already knowing the entire ecosystem.

Open source contribution must not become a way to take unlimited unpaid labour for granted. Recognition should be clear. Decision making should include sustained contributors. As resources grow, Oxy should create paid paths for work that the institution depends upon.

Disagreement is not disloyalty. A healthy community should be able to criticise leaders, architecture and policy without being treated as an enemy. Conduct rules should protect people from harassment while preserving room for serious debate.

Community building should happen around the shared Oxy mission rather than through disconnected campaigns for each application. One coherent purpose can support many invitations. Artists can enter through Syra. Sellers can enter through Mercaria. Developers can enter through the repositories. People concerned with digital rights can enter through Commons or Mention. Aligned institutions can enter through partnerships and funding.

## 11. From digital alternatives to real world responsibility

The first chapters of Oxy are technological because that is where the project began and where it has developed the greatest capacity. The mission is not limited to screens.

Over time, Oxy may support housing, education, access to food, economic participation, culture, local infrastructure, mutual aid and other services that directly affect daily life. It may create tools, operate services itself, finance community work or partner with organisations that already understand a place and its needs.

Expansion must be earned. Oxy should not make promises about housing, food or education before it has the competence, resources and accountability required to protect the people who would rely on them. It should design with affected communities rather than arriving with a universal solution created from a distance.

Digital systems should connect to material outcomes. A map is not disaster relief. A housing profile is not a home. A payment tool is not economic justice. Technology can coordinate resources, verify claims, reduce waste and give people agency, but Oxy must remain honest about the difference between enabling help and providing it.

The long term ambition is not to replace democratic society with a private authority. It is to build voluntary institutions and practical public interest infrastructure that can prove a more respectful way of organising essential parts of life.

## 12. Success, leadership and the life around the work

Oxy should be able to serve millions of people without becoming dependent on the permanent sacrifice of one person. An institution that requires its founder to work day and night forever has not yet become an institution.

Success includes real impact, financial resilience, technical reliability, community participation, trust, accessibility and the ability to continue through changes in leadership. It also includes a culture in which people can rest, care for their families and have lives beyond the mission.

Nate Isern Álvarez is the creator and founder of Oxy. The origin, persistence and direction of the project are inseparable from his work. That history should be recognised without turning the founder into an authority above the rules. His own concern about how wealth and power might change any person, including himself, is a reason to build limits now rather than after harm occurs.

Future leaders should inherit responsibility, not ownership of the mission. Selection, removal, conflicts of interest and major powers should be governed by transparent processes appropriate to the maturity of the institution.

A lasting Oxy would allow its founder to spend more time with his family because a broad community had learned to operate, challenge and improve the work together. Reduced dependence on the founder is not abandonment. It is evidence that the project has become strong enough to outlive a single life.

## 13. A living charter with protected foundations

Oxy will encounter conflicts that this document cannot predict. Ethical commitments can point in different directions. Privacy can conflict with safety. Openness can conflict with security. Local autonomy can conflict with shared rights. The institution must explain these tensions rather than pretending they do not exist.

This charter should be reviewed as Oxy grows, but change must be harder when the consequence is greater. Amendments should include a public proposal, a clear reason, consultation with affected groups, an assessment of foreseeable harm, independent review and a period in which the community can understand and challenge the change.

Certain foundations should receive the strongest available protection in future legal and governance structures. These include the refusal to sell personal data, the refusal to build the institution on advertising based surveillance, the right to meaningful transparency, the right to leave with personal data, the principle that no individual has unlimited power and the recognition that human dignity is not conditional on wealth or reputation.

Oxy should eventually express these protections not only in words, but in bylaws, ownership structures, investment terms, licences, technical architecture and enforceable community rights. A principle becomes credible when violating it is difficult, visible and costly to those who attempt it.

## Closing pledge

<Takeaways>

- Build alternatives, not slogans.
- Protect the person before the product.
- Make money without making people the product.
- Share power before power has to be taken away.
- Grow without forgetting why the work began.
- Leave behind an institution that helps people live with greater dignity than the systems it replaced.

</Takeaways>
