## Key takeaways

<Takeaways>

- Oxy is a business on purpose. A mission that cannot pay for infrastructure, security and people ends up dependent on someone whose interests are not yours.
- Revenue comes from hosting, professional services, partner programs and paid plans. It never comes from advertising, data sales or attention brokerage.
- The test for any revenue line is whether the exchange is clear, voluntary, proportionate and connected to real value or real cost.
- Surplus goes to reserves, fair pay, infrastructure, access programmes and community work. Ethical work does not require poverty from the people doing it.
- Capital can buy an economic interest. It cannot buy the right to require advertising, data sales or the removal of our open source commitments.
- The binding version of these constraints lives in the [Founding Charter](/company/charter).

</Takeaways>

## Summary

Most consumer software is free because somebody other than the user is paying, and that arrangement quietly decides everything else about the product. We would rather charge for the thing we actually do, state the price plainly, and let you leave the moment it stops being worth it.

That means Oxy has to be commercially serious. Servers cost money, security costs money, and the people doing this deserve to be paid properly and to have lives outside the work. None of that is in tension with the mission. What would be in tension is earning the money by turning the people we serve into the inventory.

This page is the practical half of that argument: the lines we sell, the ones we refuse, where the money goes, and the terms any investor has to accept.

## 1. Where the money comes from

### 1.1 Managed hosting

Teams and organisations pay us to run Oxy products on infrastructure they do not have to babysit. Self-hosting stays fully supported and fully documented: the easy path is the paid one, not the only one.

### 1.2 Professional services

Onboarding, migrations, custom integrations and long-term support for organisations running Oxy at scale. Priced transparently, quoted before the work starts, and never used as a gate in front of features that should simply exist.

### 1.3 Paid plans

Higher capacity, professional tooling and features with real operating costs behind them. A free tier has to be genuinely useful on its own, not a preview engineered to create dependency before the useful parts get removed.

### 1.4 Partner programs

Education, community and ecosystem partners building on top of Oxy. Fair revenue splits, no exclusivity traps, no lock-ins.

### 1.5 The test we apply

The question is never whether money changes hands. It is whether the exchange is clear, voluntary, proportionate and connected to real value or real cost. We do not create an obstacle and then charge you to remove it, and we do not turn safety, privacy or ordinary respect into premium features.

Where we can, pricing should account for local conditions and accessibility, and there should be ways for institutions and people with greater means to help sustain access for others.

## 2. What we refuse to earn money from

**No surveillance advertising.** We do not serve ads, we do not profile people, and we do not broker attention. Our products have never contained an ad unit.

**No data sales.** We do not sell, rent, trade or barter personal data. Not to advertisers, not to brokers, not to model trainers, not as an asset in a transaction, and not in aggregate as a workaround.

**No dark patterns.** No manufactured urgency, no hidden opt-outs, no cancellation mazes. Cancelling should take about as long as signing up.

**No closed re-licensing.** We will not take an open project, lock it behind a proprietary licence and force everyone onto a paid cloud. Open stays open.

**No paid visibility over other people.** Discovery and recommendations answer to relevance, quality and consent. Payment must not quietly become the ability to manipulate what other people see.

## 3. Where the money goes

A rough breakdown of how revenue is spent. Refined every quarter, published every year.

| Category | Share | What it covers |
|----------|-------|----------------|
| Engineering | 65% | Salaries for the people building and maintaining every Oxy product. |
| Community | 20% | Grants, contributor bounties, documentation and community programs. |
| Infrastructure | 10% | Hosting, bandwidth, third-party services and security tooling. |
| Legal and ops | 5% | Legal counsel, accounting, compliance and business operations. |

Surplus strengthens the institution rather than leaving it: reserves, fair compensation, better infrastructure, access programmes, research and direct work in communities. Fair pay, rest and long-term stability are part of the mission, not a reward for reaching it.

## 4. Capital, and the terms it has to accept

Oxy can take investment and can create a financial return for the people and institutions that help it grow. What it cannot accept is terms that give capital the right to abandon the commitments above.

An investor cannot require advertising, the sale of personal data, manipulative growth mechanics, unfair pricing, the removal of open source commitments or unilateral control over moderation and governance. Money may purchase an agreed economic interest. It may not purchase moral authority over the people Oxy serves.

We favour structures that align patient capital with long-term impact: non-voting investment, capped returns, revenue participation, mission-locked shares, public benefit structures, grants, community ownership and independent stewardship. Which mechanism fits depends on the initiative and the jurisdiction. The intended result does not change.

Financing conditions that could affect the mission get disclosed as far as law and legitimate confidentiality allow. A community cannot defend a promise it is not allowed to know exists.

## 5. How to check any of this

Read the source, read the [Transparency Center](/company/transparency), and read the [Founding Charter](/company/charter), which states these constraints in the form we intend to be held to.

If a product ever contradicts something on this page, say so publicly and name the decision. A commitment only means something when breaking it is visible.
