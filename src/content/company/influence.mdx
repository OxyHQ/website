Influence changes what people notice, believe, buy and consider possible. It can make knowledge accessible, sustain creative work, expose injustice and bring people together. It can also make deception profitable, turn vulnerability into entertainment and direct a crowd against someone who cannot answer on equal terms.

Oxy exists to build systems that respect human dignity. That purpose applies to the people who reach an audience through our services, and to the decisions through which Oxy gives some voices more visibility than others.

This declaration develops the commitments of the [Oxy Manifesto](https://oxy.so/company/manifesto/) and the [Oxy Founding Charter](https://oxy.so/company/charter/) for public expression, creators, political communication and social platforms. It is a founding draft of the standard Oxy intends to uphold. It does not certify anyone as an ethical person or claim that every safeguard described here is already operating. Product rules and implementation must make their relationship to these commitments clear.

## 1. Every person has dignity

An audience is made of people. So are the strangers in a video, the workers behind a production, the subjects of an investigation and the people who disagree in the replies.

Their dignity does not depend on popularity, nationality, race, religion, gender, sexuality, disability, wealth, political affiliation or reputation. Someone who has behaved badly still has rights. Someone who has done valuable work can still be held responsible for harm.

Oxy should assess specific conduct and its consequences. It should not turn an accusation, a mistake or a community verdict into a permanent judgment about the worth of a life.

## 2. Expression needs room to be different

People should be free to create, joke, perform, argue, experiment and live in ways others would not choose. A community that permits only comfortable opinions cannot support meaningful freedom.

Domestic life, ambition, faith, sexuality, tradition and rebellion are legitimate subjects of expression. A woman does not owe an audience a particular model of independence. A person who chooses a traditional life does not acquire the right to impose that life on everyone else. Criticism should address the expectations or claims a work promotes without prescribing how its creator must live.

Fiction, satire and performance deserve substantial freedom. A character is not automatically a confession. A staged scene need not carry a warning about every creative choice. Disclosure matters when a reasonable misunderstanding could materially affect consent, safety, spending or trust in a factual claim.

Creative freedom does not turn an unsuspecting participant into a consenting actor. Calling an event entertainment cannot erase what happened to the person inside it.

## 3. Responsibility follows power and foreseeable consequences

Influence creates an obligation to consider the people affected by its use. That obligation grows with reach, commercial interests, authority, the vulnerability of an audience and the seriousness of the consequences.

There is no follower count at which a person suddenly becomes responsible. A small account can cause serious harm. A large account can act with care. Public officials also exercise powers that an entertainer does not possess, and their obligations extend beyond the management of an audience.

The relevant questions are practical: What is being claimed or requested? Who could be affected? What does the speaker know? What could they reasonably check? Who benefits, and who bears the cost if the claim is wrong?

More responsibility must never become fewer procedural rights. Popularity provides neither immunity from rules nor a reason to presume guilt.

## 4. Trust requires honesty about facts and interests

People should distinguish what they know from what they believe, infer, perform or have been paid to present. Familiarity with a creator does not substitute for evidence.

Claims that could affect health, finances, physical safety, civic participation or the treatment of a vulnerable group require particular care. Personal experience can be meaningful without proving a general claim. An interview records what someone said; it does not automatically establish that their statement is true.

Material relationships should be understandable at the point where they affect trust. Relevant payment, ownership, gifts, employment and political coordination should not be hidden behind the appearance of an independent recommendation. A disclosure should be clear enough to understand without investigating a profile or decoding an ambiguous label.

When a consequential claim is wrong, the correction should identify the error and reach the people reasonably likely to have encountered it. Editing quietly may fix a sentence while leaving its effects untouched. A correction should be attached to the original where practical, without unnecessarily republishing private or harmful material.

## 5. Consent must survive the camera

Consent should be informed, specific and freely given. Permission to appear in one setting does not grant unlimited permission to be edited into another story, exposed to a hostile audience or reused in a commercial campaign.

The imbalance matters. A person who needs food, treatment, employment or financial help may find it difficult to refuse the person offering it. Ethical production should make refusal possible without withdrawing essential help or imposing a penalty. Agreements should explain the intended use, likely audience and realistic limits of withdrawal once material has circulated.

Public interest reporting may sometimes justify publication without a subject's permission. That judgment requires a real public interest, a proportionate intrusion and protection for people incidentally exposed. Curiosity, embarrassment and audience demand do not establish that justification by themselves.

People should have a credible way to raise concerns with someone who is not rewarded for keeping the production running at any cost.

## 6. Children deserve a future beyond the content

A child's life should not become an asset an audience feels entitled to access. Adult permission does not settle every question about a child's privacy, labour, safety or future autonomy.

Adults who create with children should protect private distress, health information, intimate moments, location and other details that could expose them to harm. They should take a child's refusal seriously, provide appropriate oversight and protect any earnings generated through the child's participation.

As children grow, they should have meaningful opportunities to challenge how their identity and childhood are used. Commercial success cannot supply the consent they were too young to give.

Oxy should build age appropriate protections without making unnecessary surveillance of children the price of safety.

## 7. Help should preserve the agency of the person receiving it

Public generosity can provide real help and encourage others to act. Its value should be described accurately, with attention to the people and organisations doing the work.

Claims about donations should explain what reaches the recipient, who pays, whether a purchase changes the donation, and what costs or conditions apply. A fixed donation should not be presented as though each sale creates additional support.

Receiving help should not require gratitude on camera, emotional disclosure or permanent loyalty to the person providing it. A beneficiary should remain free to complain about how they were treated.

Good outcomes in one part of a project do not settle questions about working conditions, safety or consent elsewhere. Oxy should apply that principle to its own community programmes as carefully as it applies it to anyone else's work.

## 8. Credit should travel with creative work

Ideas develop through influence, shared traditions, quotation, criticism, remix and collaboration. Similarity alone does not prove appropriation, and nobody owns an entire aesthetic or genre.

Creators should acknowledge identifiable contributions when they materially rely on another person's work. They should respect applicable rights, licences and permissions, and avoid presenting someone else's distinctive work as their own. Payment and credit should reflect the people who actually contributed.

Accusations of copying also require care. Identify the works, the chronology and the relevant similarities. Consider permission, common sources, independent creation and legitimate quotation before presenting a conclusion as established fact.

## 9. Political disagreement must preserve equal standing

Oxy should protect robust criticism of governments, parties, institutions, ideologies and religious doctrines. People must be able to question power, advocate change and disagree about public policy without needing the approval of Oxy's leadership.

That freedom coexists with equal dignity for the people involved. Criticising a doctrine does not establish the guilt of everyone associated with it. Debating immigration does not justify fabricated claims about a nationality. Defending women's rights or the rights of LGBTQ people must not become a pretext for denying another community's equal standing.

Threats, targeted harassment, dehumanisation and knowingly fabricated allegations deserve scrutiny whatever political purpose they serve. So do misleading edits, impersonation and concealed coordination that manufacture the appearance of independent support.

Oxy may express its own views and explain the values behind them. Participation should not depend on agreement with those views. Moderation must identify a published rule and relevant conduct; a political label alone cannot decide a case. Public importance can justify preserving material as evidence or news, while any exceptional treatment should have a reason, a defined scope and review.

## 10. A community must not become a weapon

Criticism can be strong, collective and persistent. It can include investigation, protest, calls for correction and voluntary decisions to stop supporting a creator or institution.

It should not become doxxing, threats, racial abuse, sexual humiliation or pressure against uninvolved relatives. A large audience does not confer a right to invade a small account's life. Neither does a righteous cause.

People with substantial audiences should take reasonable steps to discourage foreseeable harassment they direct or intensify. They cannot control every follower, but they remain responsible for their own instructions, endorsements and disclosures.

Oxy's institutional accounts must follow the same standard. Criticism should offer evidence and reasons without recruiting a crowd to punish a person.

## 11. Platforms are responsible for the attention they organise

A platform makes choices about what it recommends, rewards, measures and makes difficult to leave. It cannot treat every consequence of those choices as somebody else's responsibility.

Oxy should evaluate its systems for the ways they encourage repeated outrage, humiliation, dependency or misleading certainty. Popularity alone should not establish credibility. Designing for user choice also requires understandable controls and honest explanations of significant restrictions.

The Founding Charter's commitments against advertising, the sale of personal data and exploitative attention models remain in force as the direction for Oxy. This declaration creates no exception for concealed promotion or purchased influence. Ethical commerce, where a service permits it, must follow that service's published rules and preserve those commitments.

Permission to speak, eligibility for recommendation, access to a commercial programme and continued use of an account are distinct decisions. Each needs its own justification. A restriction on visibility can affect expression and livelihood; it deserves scrutiny even when a post remains accessible.

Services should disclose which controls and safeguards actually exist. A principle written on this page is not evidence that a feature has been deployed.

## 12. Consequences require a fair process

Ethical criticism, product enforcement and legal judgment answer different questions. Something may warrant criticism without warranting removal. An allegation is not a finding. A court outcome must be described accurately, including its scope and any material qualification.

Before imposing a significant restriction, Oxy should identify the rule, establish the relevant facts, consider context and choose a proportionate response. Intent, foreseeable harm, age at the time, recurrence, subsequent conduct and the reliability of the evidence all matter. No single factor should erase every other consideration.

People affected should receive understandable reasons and a meaningful opportunity to challenge the decision before a reviewer who can change it. Evidence should be preserved securely, with access limited to what is necessary. Transparency must not expose victims, confidential sources or private information to renewed harm.

Urgent protective measures may be necessary when serious harm is imminent. They should be narrow, recorded, limited in time and followed by prompt review. An emergency must not become an indefinite excuse to avoid explaining a decision.

Automated systems may help organise information, identify patterns and support accessibility. They should not become an unaccountable final judge of consequential cases. Participatory review also needs safeguards against conflicts of interest, organised capture and majority prejudice.

## 13. Accountability must leave room for repair

A responsible response to harm can include acknowledging what happened, correcting the record, returning money, compensating an affected person, changing production practices or accepting a proportionate restriction.

An apology is relevant evidence of a response. Its value grows when conduct changes and repair follows. It does not require the people affected to forgive, resume contact or restore their support.

Old conduct should be assessed in its historical context, including the person's age, later behaviour and any continuing risk. Repeatedly reviving a resolved mistake solely to humiliate someone can become another form of harm. Serious wrongdoing should not be erased merely because time has passed.

Reputation consequences should remain contextual, explainable and open to appeal. A decision about conduct on a social service should not become a universal score that determines access to housing, food, healthcare, education or basic respect elsewhere in Oxy.

## 14. Oxy must be answerable to the same principles

The founder, employees, investors, partners, reviewers and institutional accounts are all subject to these commitments. Friendship, financial importance and alignment with the mission must not purchase an exception.

Oxy should publish the rules needed to apply this declaration, disclose material gaps and report on consequential enforcement in ways that protect privacy. It should examine whether its decisions fall unevenly on particular communities and make remedies available when its own systems cause harm.

Changes to the declaration should be public, reasoned and open to challenge. Changes affecting fundamental rights should follow the stronger consultation and independent review envisaged by the Founding Charter. A new controversy should not become a shortcut for rewriting a rule against one person.

The purpose of this declaration is to make responsibility visible and contestable. People should be able to create freely, question power, receive fair treatment and continue to grow. Oxy earns trust by making those conditions real, including when doing so is inconvenient for Oxy.
